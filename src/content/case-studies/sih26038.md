title: SIH26038
claim: >-
  An explainable diabetic retinopathy screening pipeline for rural India,
  built for Smart India Hackathon 2026, that freezes its operating point
  before its sealed external test set is ever opened.
headlineMetric: Sensitivity 0.982, specificity 0.917 (95% CI, n=550), frozen 23 Aug 2026
problem: |
  Diabetic retinopathy is a leading cause of preventable blindness in India,
  where there is roughly one ophthalmologist for every 100,000 rural people
  and early screening can prevent most of the resulting vision loss.
  Problem Statement 26038, set by MathWorks for Smart India Hackathon 2026,
  names a specific failure in existing AI screening tools: they act as black
  boxes, they are validated without rigour, and they fail on the variable
  image quality that portable fundus cameras actually produce in the field.
constraint: |
  Screening for referable disease has an asymmetric cost. A missed case is a
  patient told they are fine who will not return for a year while the disease
  progresses; a false alarm is one unnecessary specialist appointment. That
  asymmetry sets a higher bar for sensitivity than for specificity, and it
  means the operating point has to be defensible under scrutiny, not merely
  accurate on average.
  With only 550 validation cases, a threshold rule that checks only the point
  estimate can pass by accident: it can certify a point whose sensitivity,
  once the sampling uncertainty is made explicit, does not actually clear the
  bar it is meant to guarantee.
decision: |
  The threshold is selected on the confidence interval, not the point
  estimate: sweep the decision threshold across the calibrated probability of
  referable disease on the validation split, and freeze the smallest
  threshold whose 95% Wilson lower bound clears both the sensitivity and
  specificity targets. That rule froze a threshold of 0.400 on 23 August
  2026, giving validation sensitivity 0.9821 (95% CI 0.9548-0.9931, 219/223)
  and specificity 0.9174 (95% CI 0.8825-0.9424, 300/327).
  Explanation is treated as a safety control, not a presentation layer: a
  Grad-CAM attention map is checked against an independent, lesion-level
  evidence channel, and a prediction the two channels disagree on is routed
  to a human rather than illustrated with a heatmap and shipped anyway.
rejectedAlternative: |
  The first threshold rule selected on the point estimate alone: reach 0.90
  sensitivity, then maximise specificity. On this model that rule selects a
  threshold of 0.620, which measures 0.9148 sensitivity, a number that reads
  as passing. Its 95% Wilson lower bound is 0.8708, short of the 0.90 target
  the rule exists to guarantee. The point-estimate rule could certify an
  operating point that the project's own reporting policy, which requires
  every figure be read as an interval, would then reject. It was replaced
  with the interval-based rule above, which is what actually produced the
  frozen 0.400 threshold.
honestLimits: |
  Every number above is measured on the internal validation split only. The
  external test set, a Messidor-2 sample from a different country and a
  different set of cameras, was placed in an access-controlled archive before
  development began and, as of this Case Study, has deliberately not been
  opened: the protocol opens it exactly once, after an operating point is
  frozen and dated, and reports whatever comes out rather than tuning until
  it looks good. There is no external number here because opening the seal
  early would have made the whole exercise meaningless.
  This is a screening aid and a research prototype, not a medical device: it
  has no regulatory clearance and is not for clinical decision-making. No
  pixel-level ground truth for neovascularisation exists in any dataset the
  pipeline was built on, so proliferative disease is never claimed as
  validated; every such prediction is escalated to a human regardless of
  confidence.
